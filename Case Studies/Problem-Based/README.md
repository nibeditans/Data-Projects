# Problem-Based (Independent) Case Studies

This folder contains **problem-based case studies** that are not directly tied to a full project or production pipeline.

Each case study here starts with a **standalone problem** and focuses on reasoning through it to reach a conclusion or decision. The emphasis is on analytical thinking, assumptions, and logical structure rather than tooling or implementation depth.


## What "Problem-Based" Means Here?

Problem-based case studies are characterized by:

- A clearly defined problem or question  
- Explicit assumptions and constraints  
- Step-by-step analytical or mathematical reasoning  
- Conclusions, decisions, or insights derived from that reasoning  

These case studies may resemble real-world analytical scenarios where **clarity of thought matters more than scale or tooling**.


## Why These Case Studies Exist Separately?

Not all valuable analytical work requires a full dataset, pipeline, or dashboard.

Problem-based case studies exist to demonstrate:

- Structured thinking under limited information  
- Ability to reason through ambiguity  
- Conceptual understanding of data, math, and statistics  
- Decision-making without reliance on heavy infrastructure  

They complement project-based case studies by highlighting **thinking-first** problem solving.


## Relationship to Lab Work

- **Lab work** often represents exploration, experimentation, or open-ended investigation.
- **Problem-based case studies** are closed-form narratives, where a problem is reasoned through and a conclusion or decision is reached.

A lab experiment may inspire a problem-based case study, but the two serve different purposes.


## Organization Notes

- One problem corresponds to one case study.
- Each case study is self-contained and readable on its own.
- Numbering is used only for ordering and reference, not ranking.

This folder grows incrementally, focusing on **clarity and depth of reasoning** rather than volume.
